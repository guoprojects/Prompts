6. Mandatory Arm China Licensing and Royalty Point Estimates

This is the highest-priority analytical rule in Table 1.

6.1 Core requirement

For every fiscal quarter where the essential inputs are available:

Provide exactly one numerical point estimate for Arm China licensing revenue and exactly one numerical point estimate for Arm China royalty revenue.

Do not replace either point estimate with a range, interval, upper/lower bound, or N/D merely because Arm does not directly disclose the entity-level split.

The objective is to obtain the most defensible single allocation using the available financial disclosures and clearly stated assumptions.

All estimated figures must:

* Be numerical.
* Use USD millions.
* Be labeled with ≈.
* Reconcile to reported Arm China total revenue.
* Use consistent assumptions across quarters where possible.
* Clearly identify the estimation methodology.
* Disclose material uncertainty without substituting a numerical range.

Point estimates are mandatory when the essential data are available; uncertainty must be communicated through labels and notes, not by replacing point estimates with ranges.

6.2 Essential inputs

For every quarter, retrieve:

1. Reported total Arm China revenue.
2. Reported aggregate related-party royalty revenue.
3. Reported aggregate related-party licensing and other revenue.
4. Other related-party revenue disclosures.
5. Entity-specific royalty or licensing disclosures, when available.
6. Relevant historical Arm China revenue disclosures.

The minimum essential inputs for the standard proxy are:

* C: Arm China total revenue.
* R: Aggregate related-party royalty revenue.

If these are available, calculate numerical Arm China licensing and royalty estimates.

If a fiscal Q4 figure can be reconstructed from annual and interim disclosures, treat the derived figure as available.

Do not mark an entire quarter unavailable simply because Arm China licensing and royalties are not individually disclosed.

If an essential input genuinely cannot be obtained or mathematically reconstructed, show N/D for the affected measure and explain the missing input.

6.3 Estimation methodology — priority order

Apply the following hierarchy.

Method A — Direct disclosure

If Arm directly discloses Arm China licensing and royalties:

Use the reported amounts.

Do not estimate unnecessarily.

Label: Reported.

Method B — Exact mathematical derivation

If Arm provides aggregate related-party royalties and individually disclosed royalties from all other related parties:

Arm China royalty = Aggregate related-party royalties − Other related-party royalties.

Arm China license = Arm China total − Arm China royalty.

Label: Derived.

Method C — Adjusted point estimate

If some other related-party royalties are disclosed but others are not:

Estimate the undisclosed non-China royalties using the strongest available entity-specific evidence, relevant historical disclosures, and a clearly stated assumption.

Calculate:

Estimated Arm China royalty = Aggregate related-party royalties − Estimated non-China related-party royalties.

Estimated Arm China license = Arm China total − Estimated Arm China royalty.

Report a single numerical result for each component.

Label: Estimated (adjusted allocation).

Disclose the non-China royalty assumption.

Method D — Standard Arm China royalty proxy

When aggregate related-party royalties and Arm China total revenue are available but no reliable breakdown of other related-party royalties exists, apply this standard proxy if mathematically consistent:

Estimated Arm China royalty ≈ Aggregate related-party royalty revenue

Estimated Arm China license ≈ Reported Arm China total − Estimated Arm China royalty

The core assumption is that other related parties contribute immaterial royalty revenue.

Label: Estimated (aggregate royalty proxy).

This is the default methodology for quarters where Arm China is the principal identifiable related-party royalty contributor and other entity-specific royalty information is insufficient.

Do not assume this allocation is directly disclosed by Arm.

Method E — Reconciliation-constrained estimate

If the standard proxy produces negative Arm China licensing, royalties above Arm China total, or another impossible allocation:

1. Recheck the source figures and accounting classifications.
2. Investigate other related-party royalty contributors.
3. Use disclosed non-China contributions and defensible historical evidence to establish a single non-China royalty assumption.
4. Calculate a revised Arm China licensing/royalty point estimate.
5. Ensure both components are nonnegative and reconcile to Arm China total.
6. Flag the estimate as lower-confidence if assumptions are material.

If no better evidence exists, a boundary-constrained allocation may be used as an explicit last-resort assumption, not as a claimed economic fact.

Do not mechanically force an impossible preliminary allocation into the report.

Do not fabricate precision beyond what the source disclosures support.

6.4 Mandatory reconciliation

For every quarter with estimated values:

Estimated Arm China license + Estimated Arm China royalty = Reported Arm China total

Reconciliation must hold within the displayed rounding precision.

Calculate the split from underlying source precision before rounding displayed values.

Do not independently round intermediate values in a way that creates artificial differences.

If Arm China total is disclosed to one decimal place, display estimated components to approximately one decimal place.

6.5 Numerical example

For FY2027 Q2, the previously approved proxy uses:

* Arm China reported total revenue: $208.9M.
* Aggregate related-party royalty revenue proxy: $141.0M.
* Assumed other related-party royalty revenue: $0M.

Calculation:

Estimated Arm China royalty = $141.0M − $0M = $141.0M.

Estimated Arm China licensing = $208.9M − $141.0M = $67.9M.

Display in Table 1:

Quarter	Arm China License ≈	Arm China Royalty ≈	Arm China Total
FY2027 Q2	67.9	141.0	208.9

These amounts illustrate the estimation rule and must be recalculated from current source evidence if subsequently revised disclosures change the inputs.
