## Objective

## 1. Reporting Window and First-Disclosure Rule

### 1.1 Two-Day Reporting Window (UTC+8)

- Determine the actual current calendar date in **UTC+8** when research begins.
- Define the reporting window as **the current calendar date and the immediately preceding calendar date in UTC+8**, inclusive.
- Search for disclosures from both dates.
- Evaluate eligibility using **calendar dates only**; do not require exact hours, minutes, or seconds.
- When reliable timestamps are available, convert them to UTC+8 to identify the appropriate calendar date, but do not perform hour-by-hour eligibility comparisons.
- Do not treat the reporting window as a strict rolling 24-hour or 48-hour interval.
- The current calendar date may be incomplete at research time; include only information already publicly available.
- Use UTC+8 consistently for all reporting dates.
- Display calendar dates only in final reports.

**Example**

If the current date in UTC+8 is October 10, 2026:

**Reporting Window: 2026-10-09 – 2026-10-10 (UTC+8)**

Include developments first disclosed on October 9 or October 10, regardless of the exact disclosure time.

Exclude developments first disclosed on October 8 or earlier unless a material new development was publicly disclosed on October 9 or October 10.

Use actual calendar dates rather than the example.

### 1.2 First Public Disclosure Determines Eligibility

Use the **first credible public disclosure date**, rather than the date of the underlying event or a subsequent media report, as the primary criterion for determining eligibility.

Include an event only when:

1. Its first credible public disclosure occurred on either of the two reporting dates; or
2. A previously disclosed event experienced a **material new development** publicly disclosed on either reporting date.

Subsequent media coverage, republication, commentary, or additional reporting on an older event does not reset its eligibility unless it reveals materially new facts.

Verify first-disclosure dates using:

- Original company announcements and press releases
- Regulatory filings and court documents
- Official conference presentations and technical publications
- Original research papers or repository releases
- The earliest credible news reports available

Distinguish between:

- **Underlying event date:** When the transaction, technical achievement, financial period, or legal event occurred
- **First public disclosure date:** When material information first became publicly available
- **Subsequent reporting dates:** When later publications discussed the information

**Date-level verification rule:**

- Verify the earliest credible public disclosure **date**, not necessarily its precise timestamp.
- Exact hours and minutes are not required to establish eligibility.
- Convert publication dates to UTC+8 when reliable timezone information is available.
- If a source provides only a calendar date without a timezone, use it provisionally and check for contradictory earlier disclosures.
- Do not assume that the date of a later news article is the first-disclosure date.
- If the first-disclosure date cannot be established reliably, identify the uncertainty and avoid presenting eligibility as certain.

Do not exclude major developments merely because the underlying financial results, transactions, or technical achievements relate to an earlier period. Their eligibility depends on **when the material information first became public**.

For a material update to an older event, identify the specific newly disclosed information and use its disclosure date.

Apply the same reporting window worldwide, including the United States, China, Taiwan, South Korea, Japan, and Europe.

Search English- and Chinese-language sources when relevant.

## 3. Research Methodology

### Step 1 — Broad, Cross-Source Discovery

Conduct independent searches across multiple source categories for developments first publicly disclosed during either reporting date.

**Mandatory source coverage**

Search major financial and general-news outlets independently, including:

- Reuters
- Bloomberg
- Financial Times
- Nikkei Asia

Also search:

1. Official company announcements, investor relations, regulatory filings, and press releases
2. Original research papers, benchmarks, standards documents, technical documentation, GitHub repositories, and conference materials
3. Specialist semiconductor and AI technology publications
4. Relevant English- and Chinese-language financial, business, and technology reporting
5. Technical-community discussions for supplementary context

**Cross-source discovery requirement**

Do not allow a technology-focused search strategy to overlook material financial, commercial, legal, regulatory, or geopolitical disclosures.

Do not rely exclusively on company announcements, semiconductor publications, or general technology-news aggregators.

Perform separate searches targeting:

- AI model and software breakthroughs
- AI compute and semiconductor architecture
- Memory, foundry, packaging, and equipment
- AI infrastructure economics, financing, earnings, and M&A
- Semiconductor IP, licensing, litigation, and regulation
- Major developments reported by financial and general-news outlets

Evaluate all eligible developments using the **same significance criteria**, regardless of their source or category.

Do not assume that an event is less important because it originated in financial reporting rather than technical reporting.

If a named outlet is inaccessible or yields no verifiable relevant results, disclose that limitation rather than implying it was comprehensively checked.

### Step 2 — Verify First Disclosure and Cross-Check Facts

For each candidate event:

1. Identify the underlying event date, if applicable.
2. Establish the earliest credible public disclosure **calendar date**.
3. Convert the date to UTC+8 when reliable timezone information is available.
4. Confirm that the first disclosure, or a material new development, occurred on either of the two reporting dates.
5. Do not require exact clock times to establish eligibility.
6. Verify important numbers, quotations, product specifications, financial details, and legal status.
7. Compare independent sources, prioritizing original evidence.
8. Distinguish confirmed information from estimates, leaks, and speculation.
9. Check whether claimed technical breakthroughs have credible supporting measurements.
10. Compare the information with previous announcements to identify genuinely material changes.
11. Reject duplicate reporting, recycled news, and unsupported promotional claims.

For major claims, seek at least two independent sources when practical.

Do not confuse multiple publications of the same announcement with independent confirmation.

If the first-disclosure date is uncertain, identify that limitation and avoid claiming confirmed eligibility.

**Important:** Search and evaluate eligibility at the **calendar-date level**. Exact disclosure hours, minutes, and seconds are not required or displayed.

### Step 3 — Prioritize by Significance

Score each eligible candidate from 1 to 5 on:

- **Technological significance:** Does it change what is technically possible?
- **Economic significance:** Could it materially affect costs, revenue, margins, industry investment, or capital allocation?
- **Strategic significance:** Could it alter competition, supply chains, licensing access, or industry structure?
- **Long-term impact:** Could its consequences persist for multiple years?

Select the **top ten most consequential events**, ordered by overall significance.

Apply the same evaluation standards to technical, financial, commercial, legal, regulatory, and geopolitical developments.

Do not artificially favor hardware announcements over major AI business developments, or vice versa.

If fewer than ten events meet the significance threshold, report only those that qualify.

### Step 4 — Critical Analysis

For each selected event, assess:

1. **What happened?** Verified facts and quantitative details.
2. **Why does it matter?** Technological, economic, and strategic significance.
3. **Who benefits or loses?** Companies, ecosystems, or supply-chain segments.
4. **What remains uncertain?** Technical, commercial, legal, or execution risks.

Clearly separate facts from analytical judgment.

Where relevant, quantify implications for compute performance, memory bandwidth, energy, infrastructure costs, revenue, capacity, or market economics.
